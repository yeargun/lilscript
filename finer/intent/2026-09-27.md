## What it asks, point by point

1. One final design, architected up front, not iteration that renames routes. The language and the compiler carry Closure ADVANCED's thought patterns *by design*, and go beyond them.
2. The main target is JavaScript, smallest under the configured compression objective (Brotli, gzip or raw). Decisions may differ per objective, and the compiler compiles differently for each.
3. Programs also compile to directly executable native code.
4. Static and semantic analysis at Closure ADVANCED depth or deeper.
5. Same or better runtime performance than the original library.
6. Beat Rolldown, Terser, Oxc and every JS toolchain's minified output on the cloned libraries, without overfitting to them.
7. Compile time is a first-class concern. Flags, effort and codec settings (compression quality included) change compile time, and the design must account for that.
